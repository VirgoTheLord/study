33 · Aptitude (Part B): Quantitative Problems

Module K · Part BNo negative marking Ratios, mixtures, percentages, scaling, equations, tables and graphs.

Start here

These are school-level maths problems wrapped in a short story. The skill is turning the words into numbers, then using a small tool. Each tool below is shown on a real paper question.

1. Ratios and mixtures

A ratio 1 : 2 means "1 part to 2 parts", so the whole is 3 parts. Always find the value of one part first. In a mixture problem, find the quantity that does not change and keep it fixed.

Worked example (2025)

45 litres of a mixture has sugar solution : salt solution = 1 : 2. How much sugar solution must be added to make the ratio 2 : 1?

  1. 3 parts = 45 litres, so 1 part = 15. Sugar = 15 litres, salt = 30 litres.
  2. Adding sugar leaves the salt fixed at 30.
  3. For ratio 2 : 1 the sugar must be twice the salt = 60 litres.
  4. Sugar to add = 60 − 15 = 45 litres.

2. Percentage change

% change = (new − old) ÷ old × 100. Divide by the old value (the starting point), not the new one.

Worked example (2023)

A table lists toy production by year for five types. For type D: 1982: 90, 1983: 105, 1984: 110, 1985: 125, 1986: 135. Percentage increase from 1983 to 1985 = (125 − 105) ÷ 105 × 100 = 20 ÷ 105 × 100 ≈ 19%. Note 20% is a trap: that would be dividing by 100.

Other forms: "x% of y" = x/100 × y. A rise of 20% then a fall of 20% does not return to the start (1.2 × 0.8 = 0.96). Profit% = profit ÷ cost price × 100.

3. Scaling: length, area, volume

If every length is multiplied by k, then area is multiplied by k² and volume by k³.

Worked example (2025)

A cube has volume 8 m³. Each side is doubled. Original side = ∛8 = 2 m. New side = 4 m, new volume = 4³ = 64 m³ (that is 8 × 2³).

4. Setting up equations

Give each unknown a letter, write one equation per fact, then combine.

Worked example (2025): who weighs what?

A + B = 150; B + C = 180; A + C + D = 360; A + B + C + D = 450. Subtract the third from the fourth: B = 450 − 360 = 90. Then A = 150 − 90 = 60, C = 180 − 90 = 90. Then D = 360 − A − C = 360 − 60 − 90 = 210 pounds.

5. Maximising area: the square is best

For a fixed perimeter P, a rectangle has the largest area when it is a square, with side P/4 and area (P/4)². (Reason: for a fixed sum a + b, the product ab is largest when a = b.)

Worked example (2023)

Perimeter 620 mm: side = 620 ÷ 4 = 155 mm. Area = 155 × 155 = 24,025 mm².

6. Reading tables and graphs

  • Read the title, axis labels and units first.
  • "Largest difference between two days" means compute the gap between each pair of neighbouring values and compare, not just the highest and lowest.
  • For tables, write out only the row/column you need before calculating.

Check yourself

ISRO 2025 · Q85

In a mixture of 45 litres, the ratio of sugar solution to salt solution is 1 : 2. What is the amount of sugar solution to be added if the ratio has to be 2 : 1?

Salt stays 30; sugar must become 60; add 60 − 15 = 45.
ISRO 2025 · Q87

Andre and Betty together weigh 150 pounds. Betty and Chris together weigh 180 pounds. Andre, Chris and David together weigh 360 pounds. Altogether the four weigh 450 pounds. How much does David weigh?

Betty = 450 − 360 = 90, Andre = 60, Chris = 90, David = 210.
ISRO 2025 · Q84 (options reconstructed by me)

A cube has a volume of 8 cubic metres. If each side is doubled in length, what will its new volume be in cubic metres?

Volume scales with the cube of the side: 8 × 2³ = 64.
ISRO 2023 · Q94

Using the toy-production table (type D: 1983 = 105, 1985 = 125), what is the approximate percentage increase in production of D type toys from 1983 to 1985?

(125 − 105) ÷ 105 × 100 ≈ 19.
ISRO 2023 · Q95

What is the maximum area of a rectangle with perimeter 620 mm?

The best rectangle is a square of side 155 mm: 155² = 24,025.
