4 · Graph Basics

Module A · Graph theoryAsked in 2023 and 2025 Vocabulary, counting formulas, planar graphs, colouring.

Start here

A graph is just dots joined by lines. The dots are vertices (or nodes); the lines are edges. Cities and roads, people and friendships, web pages and links: all graphs. Exam questions test whether you know the vocabulary and a handful of counting formulas.

Distances inside a graph

  • Eccentricity of a vertex = the distance to the vertex farthest from it.
  • Radius = the smallest eccentricity over all vertices. Diameter = the largest eccentricity.
  • Centre = the vertices whose eccentricity equals the radius. So eccentricity can equal the radius (for centre vertices).
  • Girth = the length (number of edges) of the shortest cycle.
Worked example

Take a path A–B–C–D–E (five vertices in a line). Eccentricities: A: 4 (to E), B: 3, C: 2, D: 3, E: 4. Radius = 2, diameter = 4, centre = {C}. The graph has no cycle, so no girth.

The counting formulas

1. Handshake lemma: sum of all degrees = 2 × (number of edges). Each edge adds 1 to the degree of both its ends. So the number of odd-degree vertices is always even.

2. Edges in the complete graph Kₙ: pick any 2 of n vertices: n(n−1)/2.

3. Number of different simple graphs on n labelled vertices: there are n(n−1)/2 possible edges and each is either present or absent, so 2^(n(n−1)/2).

4. A tree with n vertices has n − 1 edges.

Planar graphs and Euler's formula

When a connected planar graph is drawn with no crossings, the lines divide the paper into regions called faces. The unbounded region outside the drawing counts as one face.

Euler's formula: V − E + F = 2   (V vertices, E edges, F faces including the outer one).

Also, for a simple planar graph with at least 3 vertices, E ≤ 3V − 6. Consequences: K₅ and K₃,₃ are not planar.

Worked example (2023): faces and vertices given

A planar graph has 5 faces and 9 vertices. V − E + F = 2 → 9 − E + 5 = 2 → E = 12.

Worked example (2025): the parks question

20 malls (vertices), each reached by exactly 3 roads. Total degree = 20 × 3 = 60, so edges = 60/2 = 30 by the handshake lemma. Euler: F = 2 − V + E = 2 − 20 + 30 = 12 faces. One face is the outside, so the number of enclosed regions where a park can sit is 12 − 1 = 11.

Graph colouring

Colour the vertices so that two joined vertices never share a colour. The chromatic number is the smallest number of colours needed.

  • A graph with no edges: 1. Any bipartite graph (e.g. a tree or an even cycle): 2. An odd cycle: 3. Kₙ: n (all vertices are mutually adjacent). The Four-Colour Theorem: every planar graph needs ≤ 4.
  • The chromatic number is at least the size of the largest clique (a group of mutually adjacent vertices), and at most (maximum degree + 1).
Worked example

A 5-cycle (pentagon): colour the vertices in order 1,2,1,2 and the fifth vertex touches both a 1 and a 2, so it needs a third colour. Chromatic number = 3.

How graphs are stored

Adjacency matrix: an n×n table with 1 where an edge exists (n² memory, edge check in O(1)). Adjacency list: for each vertex, the list of neighbours (memory V + E, better for sparse graphs).

Check yourself

ISRO 2025 · Q5

There are 20 shopping malls in a city interconnected by roads. Each mall can be reached through 3 different roads. Roads do not cross each other and do not pass through a park; only one park per region. What is the maximum number of parks that can be constructed in the regions enclosed by roads?

Handshake: E = 20×3/2 = 30. Euler: F = 2 − 20 + 30 = 12. Subtract the outer face: 11.
ISRO 2023 · Q43

If there are five faces and nine vertices in an undirected planar graph, then the number of edges is

9 − E + 5 = 2, so E = 12.
ISRO 2023 · Q6

Maximum number of simple graphs possible with n vertices

n(n−1)/2 possible edges, each present or not.
ISRO 2023 · Q42

Which are true? (i) In a graph with n vertices and e edges, the sum of degrees = 2e. (ii) The eccentricity of a connected graph can never equal the radius. (iii) Girth is the total number of edges in the shortest cycle. (iv) A graph with equal degree for all vertices is a multigraph.

(i) true. (ii) false: centre vertices have eccentricity equal to the radius. (iii) true. (iv) false: that describes a regular graph. The only true statements are (i) and (iii), a combination that none of the first three options lists, so the answer is none of the above.
